# Direction B — Visual Food and Menu Experience (v1, 20260728)

Focus: food imagery, interactive menu, category browsing, mobile-kitchen ordering, daily specials, fast conversion. Built on the shared foundation.

## Rationale
The confirmed menu PDF is complete, priced and unambiguous — the strongest, most reliable asset in the whole project. Direction B makes the menu itself the hero, minimising reliance on the thin/uncertain photo library and maximising the fast, transactional mobile-kitchen journey (Journey B) the Master Brief calls out.

## One-page structure (desktop & mobile)

1. **Cinematic hero** — Full-bleed but shorter/faster than Direction A: the confirmed chef photo cropped tighter (`4:5` reframed to a wide banner) with the red duotone scrim, headline emphasising food and speed ("Kota. Chips. Cooked to order." — placeholder, real copy pending approval), primary CTA "Order now" jumps straight to the menu anchor.
2. **Lina positioning statement** — Compressed to 2–3 lines, kept above the fold's bottom edge — this direction gets to the menu fast.
3. **Mobile kitchen & catering overview** — A slim horizontal strip (not a full section): two tappable chips — "Mobile kitchen orders" / "Private catering" — routing to the menu or the enquiry form respectively.
4. **Interactive visual menu** — The largest, most elaborate section in any direction (full spec in `assets/menu/working/lina-interactive-menu-direction-v1-20260728.md`): category tabs (Plates / Kota / Chips & Snacks / Drinks) exactly mirroring the PDF, swipeable on mobile, each item a `1:1` card with price and optional special tag, tap-to-expand detail panel.
5. **Chef & trailer story** — Compressed to a single wide banner (not full-page treatment): confirmed chef photo, one line of caption, "Read more about Lina" link that anchors down rather than a full narrative block — the story exists but doesn't compete with the menu for attention.
6. **Catering occasions & services** — Revised after internal review: a four-icon card row here would read as a generic template grid, exactly the "generic dashboard/feature cards" pattern the brief prohibits. Kept as a compact single-line editorial list instead (same device as Direction A, just shorter and without imagery) — still visually lighter than A because it's one line per occasion rather than a full block, but not a card grid.
7. **Gallery/image sequence** — A tight food-first grid: the one confirmed chef photo plus placeholder tiles clearly marked "food photo pending" for each menu category (plates, kota, chips, drinks) — signalling to Tebogo exactly which shots would have the most conversion value to supply next.
8. **Order or catering enquiry** — WhatsApp-assisted order button is present persistently (sticky bottom bar, per foundation §7) throughout the menu section, not just at page end. Catering enquiry form remains available lower on the page for Journey A.
9. **Contact & social** — Same content rules as Direction A (confirmed facts only, labelled pending states for I-007/I-008).

## Motion & pacing
Faster than A: category-tab switches are instant (no transition delay beyond a quick crossfade), menu card reveals stagger quickly (60–80ms per card, capped) rather than the slow individual reveals used elsewhere. Motion here serves browsing speed, not cinematic mood.

## Strengths
- Leans entirely on the two most reliable assets (menu PDF + one confirmed photo) — lowest risk given the current asset gaps.
- Best match for the "fast WhatsApp-assisted conversion" emphasis explicitly named for this direction in the brief.
- Menu-first structure means Phase 2 additions (specials, availability toggles) slot in without a redesign.

## Risks
- Least "premium catering" credibility of the three directions — weaker fit for Journey A (weddings/funerals/corporate) if the client's ambition leans upmarket.
- Compressing the chef story to one banner may undersell the personal brand strength that Direction A highlights.
